How to Hire a Shopify Developer in Saudi Arabia in 2026

The short answer: write down what you need built before collecting quotes, expect SAR 3,000-10,000 for a solid freelancer store setup and SAR 15,000+ for custom work, and use a short list of questions to separate real developers from theme installers. Here is the honest guide.

What a Shopify developer actually does

Three different jobs hide behind one title, and paying for the wrong one is how budgets go wrong. A store setup means configuring a theme, loading your products, connecting payments and delivery, and writing policy pages - days of work, not months. A custom build is design and development: a distinct look, custom sections, a migration from another platform, or an Arabic-first store with a proper right-to-left layout. Integration work is real software: connecting the store to your inventory, accounting or courier systems. A store that only needs the first job should never be quoted for the third.

What it costs in Saudi Arabia (honest ranges)

  • Standard store setup by a freelancer: roughly SAR 3,000-10,000 depending on catalog size and how much content is written for you versus supplied by you.
  • Custom design or complex builds: commonly SAR 12,000-30,000+, with Riyadh and Jeddah agencies at the higher end and timelines measured in weeks.
  • Ongoing help: small retainers of SAR 500-2,000 per month cover updates and minor changes; many stores need far less than they are sold.

Platform costs - the Shopify plan, apps and domain - are separate and continue after launch. Read my full Shopify cost breakdown for Saudi Arabia before budgeting the build.

Local agency, local freelancer, or remote?

A Saudi agency gives you an account manager and a contract, and charges for both. A local freelancer costs less and can meet you in person. A remote specialist costs less still, and Shopify work - theme code, apps, payment setup, training on a call - happens inside a browser wherever the developer sits. The honest trade-off with remote is communication discipline: insist on a written scope, staged payments and weekly progress on a live preview link. Those three habits protect you more than any office address does.

Saudi-specific checks before you sign

  • Payments: Saudi customers expect local options - mada cards, and buy-now-pay-later services like Tabby or Tamara are common on Saudi checkouts. Gateway approval depends on your commercial registration documents, and no developer can guarantee it. Start the application early; it is the most common launch delay.
  • Arabic: many Saudi stores sell best in Arabic first. If yours will, ask every candidate to show a live Arabic store they built, on your phone, and check the right-to-left layout yourself.
  • E-invoicing: if your business is VAT-registered, Saudi e-invoicing rules apply to your orders. Confirm with your accountant what your store must produce, and agree who configures it.
  • Delivery: decide which cities you ship to and which courier handles them before the build, so shipping zones and rates are set up correctly from day one.

Questions that filter out bad hires

  • "Show me three live stores you built and exactly what you did on each." Then open them on your phone.
  • "Will the store, domain and ad accounts be registered in my business's name?" The only acceptable answer is yes.
  • "What will the store cost me every month after launch?" A developer who cannot estimate this has not run one.
  • "How do you handle payment gateway approval?" Anyone who guarantees approval before seeing your commercial registration is guessing.
  • "What is included after launch, and what is billed separately?" Get the answer in writing.

Red flags worth walking away from

Guaranteed sales or guaranteed Google rankings. A single bundled quote with no monthly breakdown. A portfolio of screenshots instead of live store links. A store built inside the developer's own Shopify account so you cannot leave. A "custom theme" that is actually a paid theme with your logo dropped in. Pressure to pay everything upfront - staged payments tied to visible progress are standard practice.

A sensible sequence

  1. Write down what you sell, roughly how many products, and who updates the store after launch.
  2. Collect two or three quotes against the same written scope, so you compare like with like.
  3. Start with a free theme and a focused catalog; upgrade only when a real limitation appears.
  4. Pay in stages: start, preview approval, launch.
  5. Keep ownership of everything - domain, store, ad accounts, analytics.

Common questions

How much does a Shopify developer charge in Saudi Arabia?

For a standard store setup - theme configured, catalog loaded, payments and delivery connected, policies written - freelancer quotes usually fall between SAR 3,000 and SAR 10,000. Custom design, Arabic-first builds or integrations commonly run SAR 12,000-30,000 or more, with agencies at the higher end. Treat any quote that cannot state the monthly running cost after launch with suspicion.

Should I hire a local Saudi developer or work remotely?

Shopify work happens almost entirely in the browser, so a remote specialist delivers the same store for less; Saudi agencies charge more and add account management. Whichever route you choose, the protections are identical: written scope, staged payments tied to visible progress, live preview links, and every account owned by your business.

Can one person handle both the store and its marketing?

Sometimes, and it is worth asking - a developer who also plans SEO and content builds a store that is easier to rank from day one. My Shopify work for Saudi Arabia and digital marketing support for Saudi Arabia are scoped separately, so you can take either or both.
