What a Saudi Shopify build covers

Catalog and buyer language

We start from your product range: how many items, how variants work, and whether buyers browse in Arabic, English or both. A Riyadh fashion label and a Dammam industrial supplier need very different catalog structures, so the collection logic is agreed before any design work begins.

Storefront and right-to-left layout

The build can cover theme setup, brand styling, home and collection layouts, product templates and mobile navigation. For Arabic storefronts the theme must handle right-to-left text properly - menus, sliders, cart drawers and checkout steps all flip, and not every theme does it cleanly. That is tested before content is loaded, not after.

Payments, shipping and tax setup

Saudi stores typically need mada acceptance alongside cards, and many buyers still prefer cash on delivery. We configure what your payment provider approves - mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay or Tabby/Tamara instalments are subject to the provider's own review of your commercial registration. Shipping zones and rates are mapped to the carriers you actually use, such as SMSA, Aramex or Saudi Post, and your ZATCA VAT number is set so invoices show the correct 15% VAT treatment.

Migration from Salla, Zid or WooCommerce

If you are moving from a Saudi platform or an older store, we review what can be exported: products, images, customer lists and order history. A redirect plan protects any search visibility the old store earned. Nothing is switched over until the new store passes its launch checks.

Handover leaves your team in control: where product data lives, which apps bill monthly, and what needs attention each week.

Saudi launch checklist

  • Arabic and English review: every customer-facing page read in both languages - navigation, product pages, cart, checkout and order emails. Machine translation left unreviewed is the fastest way to lose a buyer's trust.
  • Payment flow: a real test order through the approved provider route, covering mada and any instalment option you were approved for. Live approval stays the provider's decision.
  • Delivery promises: shipping zones match where your courier actually delivers, cash-on-delivery limits are set deliberately, and delivery timeframes on the site match what the carrier quotes for Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and beyond.
  • Tax display: prices show whether 15% VAT is included, and the VAT number appears where Saudi buyers and the ZATCA e-invoicing rules expect it.
  • Mobile first: most Saudi shoppers will meet the store on a phone, so menus, filters, variant pickers and checkout are checked on a small screen before anything else.
  • Ownership: the business keeps control of its Shopify account, domain and billing; access is granted only as needed.

Anything that depends on a payment provider, courier or translator is flagged as an external dependency in the plan, not silently assumed.

Common questions from Saudi store owners

Can you move my store from Salla or Zid to Shopify?

Usually yes. We first review what the current platform can export and what has to be rebuilt by hand, then agree the migration scope before anything is switched over.

Will my Arabic storefront look right on Shopify?

With the right theme and proper right-to-left testing, yes. The theme choice is made around your Arabic content, not adjusted for it afterwards.

Can you guarantee mada or Tabby approval?

No. Payment providers approve businesses based on their own criteria, including your commercial registration. I set up and test what they approve - I do not promise their decision.
